## Overview

On July 29, 2024, the National Bank of Ethiopia announced that the Birr would shift to a market-determined exchange rate — ending two decades of managed peg that had kept the official rate artificially stable. The immediate effect was dramatic: the Birr moved from approximately 57 ETB/USD to over 110 ETB/USD within weeks, and has continued to weaken to approximately 150 ETB/USD by late 2025.

For Ethiopian EV importers, this represents a fundamental structural change to the business. Payment for Chinese EVs happens in USD (or RMB). Revenue is collected in ETB. The business sits across a currency pair that has moved 160% in 18 months.

This article provides a practical framework for understanding the currency risk and structuring pricing to protect margins.

---

## The Three-Phase Currency Story

### Phase 1: The Peg Era (Pre-July 2024)
The National Bank of Ethiopia maintained a managed exchange rate that moved slowly and predictably — approximately 2–5% per year in annual adjustments. For EV dealers, this meant that USD-denominated costs could be planned against ETB revenue with reasonable confidence.

The peg created distortions: the official ETB/USD rate was significantly stronger than the parallel market rate. For dealers accessing forex through official banking channels, the peg provided USD at below-market rates. For those relying on the parallel market, the gap between official and parallel rates created both risk and opportunity.

### Phase 2: The Float and Initial Shock (July 2024–December 2024)
The July 2024 float removed the managed rate. The official exchange rate aligned with (and partially overtook) the parallel market rate within days. Importers who had planned USD costs using the old official rate found their ETB-denominated margins compressed immediately.

The IMF and World Bank supported the float as part of Ethiopia's broader economic reform package, which included debt restructuring discussions and budget support — the policy shift was not reversed.

### Phase 3: Continued Weakening and New Normal (2025–2026)
The Birr has continued to weaken from the initial float. By late 2025, the rate had reached approximately 150 ETB/USD. Some stabilisation has occurred, but currency analysts and the IMF program documents do not project a return to the pre-float rate. The 150 ETB/USD range, with continued gradual weakening, is the operating assumption for EV importers planning 2026 business.

## The Impact on EV Dealer Economics

A concrete example illustrates the scale of the problem:

**Scenario**: An importer ordered a batch of BYD Seagulls in early 2024, when the ETB/USD rate was 57. Expected landed cost per vehicle was approximately $20,000 USD × 57 = ETB 1,140,000. They priced the vehicle at ETB 1.35 million for a ~18% margin.

By the time those vehicles were sold (3–4 months later, after shipping, clearance, and dealer setup), the rate was 110 ETB/USD. Their replacement cost for the next batch was approximately $20,000 USD × 110 = ETB 2,200,000 — nearly double the original ETB pricing. Their ETB 1.35 million selling price was now below their replacement cost.

This scenario played out for dealers across Ethiopia in H2 2024. Those who had not built currency protection into their model, or who had priced inventory before the float without hedging, suffered significant losses.

## The Pricing Framework for 2026

Three principles define a sustainable pricing strategy in a weakening currency environment.

### Principle 1: Price in USD, Collect in ETB at Spot

Rather than setting an ETB price that becomes stale as the exchange rate moves, price each vehicle at the USD equivalent cost-plus-margin, and convert to ETB at the prevailing NBE rate on the day of the sales contract.

In practice: state your vehicle price as **"ETB equivalent of $26,500 USD at the prevailing NBE rate on the date of agreement"**. This transfers the currency movement risk to the buyer for the period between quotation and purchase — which is fair and transparent, and increasingly standard practice among Ethiopian EV dealers.

Resistance from buyers who want a fixed ETB price is normal. The counter-argument: a fixed ETB price that embeds a large currency depreciation buffer is more expensive than a USD-pegged price, because you must price conservatively to protect yourself.

### Principle 2: Minimise the Gap Between USD Payment and ETB Collection

The longer the gap between when you pay USD for a vehicle and when you collect ETB from the buyer, the longer your currency exposure window. The operational goal is to **compress the gap** — ideally, collect payment in ETB before or simultaneously with your USD payment clearing for the inventory.

Options:
- **Pre-sale model**: collect deposit (30–50%) from buyer before placing the import order; use this to fund working capital
- **Consignment financing**: where a buyer commits to purchase before the vehicle ships, collecting ETB commitment early
- **Faster clearance**: every day a vehicle sits at Djibouti or on the road to Addis is a day of currency exposure; investing in clearance speed directly reduces currency risk

### Principle 3: Transparent Currency Pass-Through Communication

Ethiopian commercial buyers understand the Birr's weakness — they are managing the same risk in their own businesses. Transparent communication about how your pricing reflects currency movements builds trust rather than eroding it.

A dealer who explains: "Our vehicle price is based on the USD cost of importing from China, and we adjust the ETB price to the current NBE rate at time of sale" is more credible than one who posts a fixed ETB price and then either raises it unexpectedly or absorbs a loss silently.

## Working with Ethiopian Banks for Forex Access

The July 2024 float changed the mechanics of forex access as well as the rate. Pre-float, importers accessed subsidised official-rate forex through NBE-licensed banks — allocation was limited and queued. Post-float, forex is available at market rates through licensed banks, but volume constraints and bank-specific liquidity conditions still affect access timing.

Key considerations for EV importers:
- **Use a bank with established trade finance capability**: Commercial Bank of Ethiopia (CBE), Awash Bank, and Dashen Bank have more active trade finance desks than smaller regional banks
- **Documentary Letter of Credit (LC) is the preferred payment instrument**: Chinese exporters strongly prefer LC for first-time transactions; it also protects you against non-delivery
- **Plan 2–4 weeks for forex clearance**: even at market rates, the paperwork and compliance process for releasing forex for vehicle imports takes time
