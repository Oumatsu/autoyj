## Overview

China's EV price war is not a media narrative — it is a documented, sustained market phenomenon that has reshaped the economics of Chinese EV exports to emerging markets. Since early 2023, the average selling price of new energy vehicles in China has been on a sustained downward trend — with industry media reporting consecutive months of decline exceeding two years — driven by intense domestic competition between more than 30 active EV brands fighting for market share.

The cumulative effect has been significant: multiple media trackers and industry analysts report that BYD's average selling price has fallen substantially between 2023 and 2026, with some estimates citing declines exceeding 30% across its product range. Across the sector, entry-level EV price points that once required a premium have migrated to mainstream pricing.

For African importers, understanding this price trend — and its driving forces — is essential for both sourcing strategy and pricing conversations with Chinese suppliers.

---

## What Is Driving the Price War

### Overcapacity in Chinese Manufacturing

China's government-supported EV investment created manufacturing capacity that significantly exceeded domestic demand. More than 30 EV brands entered production between 2020 and 2024. Not all can survive — the price war is partly an attrition exercise, with brands cutting prices to maintain volume and cash flow while weaker competitors are forced out.

Consequence for African importers: the brands that survive this price war are the structurally stronger ones — BYD, Geely, Chery, SAIC (MG), Changan. These are also the brands with the most credible Africa-facing export infrastructure.

### Battery Cost Decline

LFP battery cells, which power the majority of China's mainstream EV output, declined in cost from approximately $150/kWh in 2022 to approximately $70–$80/kWh by early 2026. This is driven by CATL and BYD's manufacturing scale and supply chain integration. Lower battery costs directly reduce vehicle manufacturing costs, enabling lower selling prices without sacrificing margin at the same rate.

### Subsidy Phase-Out and Competitive Response

China's national NEV purchase subsidies ended in January 2023. Manufacturers faced a choice: allow demand to decline as the subsidy-adjusted price rose, or cut prices to maintain the effective purchase cost for buyers. Most chose the latter.

## How China's Domestic Price War Translates to FOB Export Pricing

The relationship between China domestic retail price and FOB export price is not direct, but it is correlated. When domestic prices fall, export pricing typically follows within 6–12 months as export channel margins compress.

The mechanism: Chinese export traders compete for orders from African importers in the same way Chinese domestic dealers compete for retail buyers. When BYD cuts its Chinese domestic price by 10%, it creates margin pressure on export traders who are offering the same vehicle at a fixed FOB price. Over time, this compresses FOB pricing.

**Practical implication**: if you quoted a vehicle FOB in Q3 2025 and received what seemed like a firm price, the market conditions in Q1 2026 may allow you to renegotiate downward — particularly for models where the domestic price has moved significantly.

**Important caveat**: FOB export prices are also affected by factors that move independently of domestic price:
- Chinese export rebates (tax rebate on exports): changed by Chinese government policy
- Currency movements (RMB vs USD)
- OEM export documentation costs (increased post-180-day rule)
- Shipping lane surcharges (Red Sea impact)

Do not assume domestic price declines translate immediately and fully to your FOB quote. But use them as a negotiation anchor.

## Which Brands Have Cut Most Aggressively

**BYD** has cut prices most aggressively and most publicly, including multiple rounds of price reductions on the Seagull, Dolphin, and Atto 3 between 2023 and 2026. BYD's scale gives it the cost structure to cut while remaining profitable — which it has been.

**Chery** has maintained competitive pricing on its export lineup, including the iCar range, and has been particularly aggressive on African market pricing for its PHEV models.

**SAIC (MG)** reduced MG4 pricing in the UK and European markets significantly in 2024-2025, with some of those reductions flowing through to export pricing on other channels.

**Smaller brands** have cut most desperately — but their deeper cuts often reflect underlying financial stress rather than genuine structural cost advantage. A 40% price cut from an unknown brand may be commercially tempting but carries supply chain continuity risk.

## What This Means for Your Procurement Strategy

**1. Annual pricing reviews are no longer sufficient.** In a 32-month consecutive price decline environment, a FOB price locked 18 months ago is likely above current market. Review your supplier prices every 6 months and benchmark against current CIF data from other importers in your market.

**2. Volume commitment unlocks the best pricing.** Chinese export traders and OEM regional export offices both offer significant discounts for committed volumes — typically 5+ units per order. If you can commit to 10+ units, you should expect negotiated pricing materially below standard quote.

**3. Payment terms are a pricing lever.** Chinese exporters prefer LC (Letter of Credit) or wire transfer against BL. Buyers who offer faster payment (T/T with higher deposit) sometimes access better pricing than those requiring extended payment terms.

**4. Watch for brand consolidation.** As weaker brands exit the market, the supply of vehicles from those brands becomes uncertain. Focus on brands with confirmed production continuity and financial stability — BYD, Geely, Chery, SAIC are the safest bets for long-term supply relationships.
