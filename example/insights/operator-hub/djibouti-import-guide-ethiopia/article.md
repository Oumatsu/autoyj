*Djibouti is Ethiopia's only viable maritime gateway. Every vehicle imported into Ethiopia passes through Djibouti — through the Doraleh Multi-Purpose Port (RoRo) or the Doraleh Container Terminal. Understanding the port system, the clearance process, and the transit corridor is essential operational knowledge for any Ethiopian EV importer.*

---

## Overview

There is no choice of port for Ethiopian importers. Ethiopia is landlocked. Eritrea's Massawa — once Ethiopia's primary port — has been inaccessible since the 1998–2000 war. Somalia's Berbera is occasionally used but lacks the infrastructure volume. **Djibouti handles over 95% of Ethiopia's seaborne trade**, including all vehicle imports of any volume.

This is not a disadvantage the market has not adapted to. Djibouti's port infrastructure, particularly the Doraleh Multi-Purpose Port developed in partnership with DP World, is one of the most modern in East Africa. The Addis-Djibouti transport corridor — road and rail — is well-established. The question is not whether to use Djibouti, but how to use it efficiently.

---

## The Djibouti Port System

Djibouti has four major port facilities relevant to vehicle imports:

### Doraleh Multi-Purpose Port (DMPP)
The primary RoRo vehicle terminal. Operated by Doraleh Multipurpose Port SA with concession arrangements with international operators. Handles RoRo vehicle imports from China, the Middle East, and Europe. Dedicated vehicle storage yard and RoRo berth. This is where Chinese EV RoRo shipments arrive.

### Doraleh Container Terminal (DCT)
Operated by DP World under a long-term concession. Handles standard ISO container traffic. Vehicles imported in containers clear through DCT. High-capacity gantry cranes; one of the most efficient container terminals in the region.

### Djibouti Port (Old Port / Port de Commerce)
The older, smaller facility handling general cargo and some bulk freight. Less relevant for Chinese EV vehicle imports.

### Djibouti Free Zone (DFZ)
A special economic zone adjacent to the port. Some importers use the free zone for temporary storage and transshipment. For direct Ethiopia-bound imports, the free zone is generally not necessary.

## The Ethiopian Customs Process at Djibouti

**Key structural fact**: Ethiopian customs authority (ERCA — Ethiopian Revenues and Customs Authority) maintains a liaison and processing presence **at Djibouti port**. Ethiopian customs clearance begins at the port, not at the Ethiopia-Djibouti border.

This matters operationally: once a vehicle has been released by Ethiopian customs at Djibouti, it moves to Ethiopia under a transit declaration — it does not face substantive re-clearance at the Galafi or Dewele border crossings. The border crossings are documentary checks, not a second customs clearance.

### The Clearance Sequence

**Step 1: Pre-arrival document submission**
ERCA uses the ASYCUDA World system for customs declarations. Ethiopian importers (or their customs clearing agents in Djibouti) can submit an import declaration and supporting documents before the vessel arrives. Pre-arrival processing compresses clearance time significantly — vehicles with complete, pre-submitted documentation can be released within 5–7 working days of arrival; those processed only after arrival typically take 10–15 working days.

Documents to submit pre-arrival:
- Bill of Lading (telex release or original)
- Commercial Invoice
- Packing List
- Certificate of Origin
- Pre-Shipment Inspection certificate (if required by ERCA for the specific vehicle type)
- OEM After-Sales Confirmation Document (for vehicles <180 days old — new January 2026 requirement)
- Bank transfer documentation / Letter of Credit evidence

**Step 2: Customs duty assessment**
ERCA calculates duties based on the CIF value (Cost + Insurance + Freight at Djibouti). Disputes about CIF valuation are the most common cause of clearance delays. Under-invoiced commercial invoices are flagged against ERCA's reference price database — bring this to the correct CIF value before submission, not as a response to a query.

**Step 3: Duty payment**
Duties are paid through an ERCA-designated bank in Ethiopia or through the Ethiopian banking system's forex mechanism. Payment clearance confirmation is required before physical release. This step introduces banking processing time — typically 2–5 business days if initiated promptly.

**Step 4: Physical inspection (selective)**
ERCA performs physical inspection on a random or risk-based selection of vehicles. If your vehicle is selected, expect 1–3 additional days for inspection scheduling and execution. Inspection checks VIN against documentation, general condition, and compliance with stated specifications.

**Step 5: Release and transit documentation**
Once duties are paid and any inspection is complete, ERCA issues the release note. The vehicle is cleared for transport to Ethiopia under an ERCA transit declaration. The transport declaration accompanies the vehicle to the border crossing and to the destination in Ethiopia.

## The Addis-Djibouti Corridor: Road vs Rail

### Road Transport (Dominant Method)

The Addis Ababa–Djibouti road corridor is approximately 900 km, passing through Dire Dawa and the Afar region. Transit time is typically 1–3 days depending on the vehicle type, carrier, and road conditions.

Vehicle-specific transport:
- **Low-loader or car carrier trucks** are used for individual or small-batch vehicle transport (2–8 vehicles)
- **Drive-off (self-drive) under escort** is possible but uncommon for Chinese EVs (requires confirmed battery state and EV-trained driver)

Key considerations:
- Fuel for the transport truck is a cost component; rates vary with diesel prices
- Road quality varies on the Addis-Djibouti route; some sections are unpaved or under repair
- Crossing the Ethiopia-Djibouti border at Galafi requires carrier documentation and transit permits

**Typical road transport cost**: ETB 8,000–15,000 per vehicle ($55–$100 USD) for the Djibouti–Addis Ababa corridor. Rates vary with load size, negotiation, and seasonal fuel surcharges.

### Addis-Djibouti Railway (Alternative)

The Addis Ababa–Djibouti Railway (ADR) — a 756 km electrified standard-gauge railway completed in 2017 — provides an alternative to road transport for containerised freight. The railway connects Djibouti port directly to the Lebu ICD (Inland Container Depot) in Addis Ababa.

For vehicle imports: container freight moves by rail if the importer opts for container shipping. RoRo vehicles are typically transported by road carrier, as rail requires vehicles to be container-loaded at Djibouti — adding cost and complexity.

Rail transit time: 12–18 hours Djibouti to Addis Ababa (versus 1–3 days by road), but scheduling and availability constraints mean the effective door-to-door time advantage is smaller than the transit time difference suggests.

## The Customs Broker: Your Most Important Local Partner

Ethiopian importers without deep experience in Djibouti-based clearance should retain a licensed Djibouti customs clearing agent (agent en douane). This is not optional — it is the difference between a 7-day clearance and a 30-day clearance.

What to look for in a Djibouti clearing agent:
- Licensed by Djibouti Customs Authority (Direction des Douanes)
- Experience specifically with vehicle imports — not just general freight
- Established relationship with ERCA's Djibouti liaison office
- Transparent fee structure (clearing agent fees typically range from $150–$400 per vehicle)

Your Chinese freight forwarder may recommend a clearing agent. This is a viable starting point, but verify the agent's track record independently if possible. A poorly performing clearing agent creates delays that are invisible to your Chinese supplier but very visible to you.

## Common Causes of Clearance Delays — and How to Avoid Them

| Cause | Prevention |
|-------|-----------|
| CIF value dispute | Submit invoices at accurate CIF; do not under-invoice |
| Missing OEM document (post Jan 2026) | Obtain before vessel sails; cannot be sourced retroactively |
| BL consignee mismatch | Verify BL consignee matches ERCA-registered importer name exactly |
| Battery SoC not documented | Request battery SoC document from shipper before departure |
| Delayed duty payment | Initiate bank forex process before vessel arrives at Djibouti |
| PSI report missing or from unapproved inspector | Confirm ERCA-approved inspector list before shipment |
