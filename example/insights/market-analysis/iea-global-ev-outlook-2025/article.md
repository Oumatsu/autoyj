## Overview

The International Energy Agency's **Global EV Outlook 2025**, published in May 2025, is the most comprehensive annual survey of electric vehicle markets worldwide. Drawing on data from governments, manufacturers, and energy system operators across more than 50 countries, it provides the closest thing the industry has to a definitive market benchmark.

For African EV dealers and fleet operators, the report contains both sobering baseline data and genuinely exciting trajectory projections. This analysis extracts the most commercially relevant findings.

---

## The Africa Headline: Doubled, But Still Under 1%

The most-cited Africa data point from the IEA 2025 report:

> **Electric car sales in Africa more than doubled in 2024, reaching nearly 11,000 units. The EV sales share remains below 1% of total new vehicle sales.**

That combination — strong growth rate, tiny absolute penetration — defines the Africa opportunity. A market that sold fewer than 11,000 EVs in 2024 across a continent of 1.4 billion people, in a vehicle sector dominated by second-hand imports, is not yet a scale market. But it is moving.

The growth was geographically concentrated. **Morocco and Egypt** each exceeded 2,000 EV sales in 2024, together accounting for the majority of the continent's growth. Sub-Saharan Africa — where Evexportweb operates — remains in earlier-stage territory, with Kenya, South Africa, and Ethiopia as the primary active markets.

## The Global Context: Why Africa's Timing Is Right

The IEA report's broader findings set a critical context for understanding why African dealers are entering this market at an advantageous moment.

### Global EV Sales Exceeded 17 Million in 2024

Total global EV sales surpassed 17 million units in 2024, with the EV share of new car sales reaching approximately 20% globally. China alone accounted for more than 11 million of those sales — meaning China's domestic market is not just large, it is the production and technology base from which African imports draw.

### Chinese Imports Drive 75% of Emerging Market EV Growth

Across all emerging economies outside of China, **Chinese imports accounted for 75% of the increase in EV sales in 2024**. In Brazil and Thailand — the most advanced emerging EV markets — Chinese vehicles represented 85% of all EV sales.

This is not a coincidence. Chinese OEMs have built the most cost-competitive EV supply chain in the world, and they are pricing aggressively in markets where they are building brand presence. African dealers who work with Chinese manufacturers are accessing this global cost leadership directly.

### The Emerging Market Surge: 60%+ Growth in 2024

In emerging and developing economies across Asia, Latin America, and Africa combined, EV sales grew by over **60% year-on-year in 2024**, with the EV sales share nearly doubling from 2.5% to 4%.

Africa's sub-1% share means it is significantly behind this emerging market average — which is either a problem or an opportunity, depending on your time horizon. Dealers building market position today are doing so before the adoption curve inflects upward.

## The Battery Chemistry Finding

The IEA report confirms a finding that directly validates sourcing strategy: **LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate) battery chemistry is increasingly dominant in commercial and emerging market applications**, driven by its lower cost, thermal stability, and longer cycle life.

Chinese manufacturers — BYD, CATL, SAIC, and others — produce the majority of the world's LFP battery capacity. This means African dealers sourcing Chinese EVs are automatically accessing the most durable and cost-effective chemistry for equatorial operating conditions.

## The 2030 Projection: A Useful Caution

The IEA's projections for African EV market growth to 2030 vary significantly depending on policy assumptions. Under optimistic scenarios with sustained government incentives, Africa's EV fleet could reach several hundred thousand units by 2030. Under conservative scenarios with limited policy support, growth remains slow.

The key variable is policy stability — exactly the theme this Insights series addresses. Dealers who monitor policy environments closely and build diversified market exposure across multiple African countries are best positioned to benefit from whichever scenario unfolds.
