## Overview

The IEA's Global EV Outlook 2025 contains a data point that deserves more attention than it has received: **across all emerging economies outside China, Chinese-manufactured EVs accounted for 75% of the total increase in electric car sales in 2024**.

In Brazil and Thailand — currently the most advanced emerging EV markets outside China — Chinese imports represented **85% of all EV sales**. In Indonesia, Malaysia, and Mexico, the figure was 60%.

Africa is still in earlier stages of this transition, but the directional signal is clear. When emerging markets electrify, they do so overwhelmingly with Chinese vehicles. African dealers who build their sourcing relationships and technical competency around Chinese OEMs are not taking a bet on the unknown — they are positioning for the dominant outcome that has already materialised everywhere else.

---

## Why Chinese OEMs Win in Emerging Markets

The 75% figure is not a coincidence or a temporary market anomaly. It reflects structural advantages that have compounded over a decade of policy-driven investment.

### Price Leadership

The domestic price competition in China's NEV market — where more than 30 brands are competing aggressively — has driven vehicle costs to levels that no other country's manufacturers can match. A capable urban EV from a credible Chinese brand can be sourced for $10,000–$18,000 FOB, a price point that is simply unavailable from Korean, Japanese, or European manufacturers.

### LFP Battery Cost Advantage

Chinese manufacturers, led by CATL and BYD, produce the majority of the world's LFP battery capacity. LFP chemistry is cheaper to produce than NCM, more thermally stable, and better suited to equatorial operating conditions. Chinese OEMs have integrated this advantage vertically — controlling the battery supply chain gives them a cost floor that foreign manufacturers cannot undercut.

### Speed of Model Development

Chinese OEMs are releasing new models at a pace that no Western manufacturer matches. BYD alone introduced more than a dozen distinct models across its product line in 2024–2025. This rapid iteration means African dealers can access current-generation technology, not vehicles designed for a different market and adapted as an afterthought.

## The Brand Landscape: Who Is Active in Africa

Not all Chinese OEMs are equally committed to the African market. Understanding which brands have genuine Africa strategies — versus those treating the continent as an overflow destination for unsold inventory — is critical for dealers choosing long-term partners.

### BYD
The world's largest EV manufacturer by volume is the most actively Africa-focused Chinese brand. South Africa has been the primary anchor market, with BYD planning to expand its dealer network from 13 to 60–70 by end of 2026. BYD's Africa strategy is explicitly long-term — they are building service infrastructure, not just selling units.

**Models relevant to African markets:** Seagull (entry commercial), Dolphin (mid-range), Atto 3 (compact SUV), Seal (sedan), Sealion 7 (large SUV), Shark 6 (plug-in hybrid pickup — relevant for East Africa).

### Chery
Chery's expansion into South Africa is accelerating, with eight hybrid and plug-in hybrid models being introduced in 2025–2026, plus a commitment to bring its full EV range (iCar brand) to the market. Chery has an existing dealer infrastructure in several African markets through its ICE vehicle business, making the EV transition operationally smoother than for brands entering from scratch.

### SAIC (MG)
MG — technically a British brand now owned by SAIC — has a significant global EV presence and is present in South Africa. The MG4 and ZS EV are among the more recognised Chinese-origin EVs on the continent. SAIC operates a global after-sales parts network serving international markets, with European regional distribution infrastructure that supports Africa-facing supply chains through its MG authorised importer programme.

### Geely / Geometry
Geely's Geometry brand is specifically positioned for emerging markets at competitive price points. Less visible in Africa currently than BYD or Chery, but has a manufacturing base and supply chain that supports RHD production for right-hand-drive African markets.

## What Dealers Should Evaluate

When choosing which Chinese OEM to partner with for long-term sourcing, the 75% market share figure is useful context — but the operational questions matter more:

- **Does the OEM produce native RHD units**, or are vehicles converted after production? (Warranty and safety implications)
- **What is the after-sales parts supply chain for your specific market?** Spare parts availability within 30 days is a minimum viable standard for a dealership.
- **Does the brand have a regional export office** that can provide documentation, technical support, and priority production allocation?
- **What is the warranty policy for export vehicles**, and does it apply in your country of operation?

The brand with the best marketing profile is not always the best sourcing partner. Operational depth matters more than brand awareness in markets where the after-sales experience will make or break your reputation.
