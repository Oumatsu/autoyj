## Overview

When a Chinese EV leaves a factory in Shenzhen, Tianjin, or Chongqing headed for East or West Africa, it will move by one of two ocean freight methods: **RoRo (Roll-on Roll-off)** or **container**. Both deliver vehicles. They differ substantially in cost, risk profile, loading process, transit time, and the type of port infrastructure they require at the receiving end.

Understanding this choice is not an abstraction — it affects your per-unit landed cost, your insurance exposure, and the condition of vehicles when they arrive. This article provides the framework for making the right decision based on shipment size, destination port, and vehicle type.

---

## How RoRo Works

RoRo ships are purpose-built vehicle carriers — also called Pure Car and Truck Carriers (PCTCs). Vehicles drive onto the vessel under their own power or are towed using low-profile trailers, then secured to the deck with straps and wheel chocks. At the destination port, vehicles drive or are towed off.

RoRo is the dominant method for new vehicle manufacturer export worldwide, including virtually all Japanese used vehicle exports to East Africa that have defined the industry for 30 years. The largest RoRo ships carry up to 7,000 CEUs (Car Equivalent Units).

**RoRo loading requirements**: vehicles must be driveable or towable, fuel tanks must be below a quarter tank for fire safety compliance, and batteries in EVs must typically be at 30–50% charge at loading. Batteries at 0% or 100% charge may face loading restrictions at some RoRo terminals.

## How Container Shipping Works

Container shipping uses standard ISO 20-foot or 40-foot boxes. Vehicles are driven into containers at the loading end, secured with wheel chocks and tie-down straps, and sealed for ocean transit. At the destination, vehicles are lifted off and driven out.

A standard 40-foot container fits one mid-size sedan or SUV with room for accessories. Some configurations allow two compact vehicles (like the BYD Seagull or MG4) with careful arrangement. A 20-foot container fits one compact vehicle.

Container shipping requires additional labour at both ends for loading and unloading, and specialised container handling equipment at the destination port. However, it provides the vehicle with a physical enclosure that protects against weather, theft, and damage during transit.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Factor | RoRo | Container |
|--------|------|-----------|
| Cost per vehicle | Lower (no container rental, simpler handling) | Higher (container rental + lashing + handling) |
| Transit time | Typically 20–35 days China → East Africa | Similar; +2–5 days for container port logistics |
| Vehicle exposure | Open deck; weather and salt spray exposure | Enclosed; fully protected from elements |
| Minimum shipment | Single vehicle (most RoRo operators) | Single vehicle (one container) |
| Bulk discount | Yes — significant savings at 10+ units | Less pronounced |
| EV battery risk | Battery at 30-50% charge required | Same restriction applies |
| Port compatibility | Requires dedicated RoRo berth | Any container terminal |
| Theft risk in transit | Low (vessels are managed) | Low (containers are sealed) |
| Condition on arrival | Generally excellent; minor dust | Excellent; fully enclosed |

## When to Choose RoRo

RoRo is typically the right choice when:

**You are shipping 3 or more vehicles in a single consignment.** The per-unit cost advantage of RoRo over container becomes material at volume. Logistics operators report RoRo rates approximately 15–25% lower than equivalent container rates per vehicle at scale, on the China–East Africa lane.

**Your destination port has a dedicated RoRo terminal.** Djibouti's Doraleh Multi-Purpose Port has an established RoRo berth with regular services from Grimaldi, UECC, and China-Africa dedicated carriers. Mombasa's Kilindini Harbour handles RoRo regularly. These ports represent the easiest RoRo clearance experience in East Africa.

**You are shipping standard passenger EVs.** RoRo is ideal for sedans, hatchbacks, and SUVs — the core of the Chinese EV product range heading to Africa.

**You are prioritising cost over enclosure.** For new vehicles with no particular sensitivity to deck exposure, RoRo condition outcomes are generally indistinguishable from container.

## When to Choose Container

Container is typically right when:

**You are shipping one or two vehicles** and cannot fill a RoRo vessel's minimum lot requirement — or when your freight forwarder's RoRo consolidation timeline does not match your delivery schedule.

**Your destination port has limited RoRo infrastructure.** Dar es Salaam's RoRo capacity is constrained; Tema (Ghana), Apapa (Lagos), and several West African ports handle container vehicles more reliably than RoRo.

**You are shipping premium or show vehicles** where any surface exposure risk is unacceptable — for example, vehicles destined for a showroom reveal.

**You are shipping modified vehicles** or vehicles with non-standard components that may have specific drivability issues at loading.

## The EV-Specific Consideration: Battery State at Loading

This is the one area where EV shipping differs from ICE vehicle shipping and where importers consistently encounter unexpected costs or delays.

Most RoRo terminals and container yards require EV batteries to be at **30–50% state of charge at loading**. Vehicles arriving at the terminal with depleted or fully charged batteries may be refused loading or subject to on-site charging/discharging that adds cost and delay.

Chinese exporters working with legitimate OEM channels typically manage this — vehicles are parked at export yards for days to weeks, and charge levels are monitored. Grey-market traders moving vehicles directly from registrations to ports may deliver vehicles at 0% charge (never been driven, never been charged since factory). This creates a loading issue that is your problem to solve, not theirs.

**Recommendation**: confirm battery state of charge protocols with your freight forwarder before accepting a shipment. Request documentation of battery SoC at loading for any EV shipment.
