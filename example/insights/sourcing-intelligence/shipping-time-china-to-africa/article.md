## Overview

"How long does shipping from China to Africa take?" is the most searched question among first-time African EV importers — and the most consistently underestimated in the answers that circulate online.

Freight marketing materials quote ocean transit times of 20–28 days. This is technically accurate for the ship-at-sea phase. The complete journey — from the moment a vehicle leaves a Chinese factory gate to the moment it is available for sale at your dealership — typically takes **45 to 90 days**, with the longer end of that range applying to Ethiopia-bound shipments and periods of elevated port congestion.

Understanding each phase of this timeline is essential for managing working capital, setting customer expectations, and planning inventory cycles.

---

## The Five Phases of China-to-Africa Vehicle Transit

### Phase 1: Factory Gate to Chinese Export Port
**Typical duration: 3–10 days**

After a vehicle leaves the production line, it moves to a holding yard at or near the export port. In China, the major vehicle export ports are:
- **Tianjin** (Northern China; primary for Beijing-area OEMs and certain BYD facilities)
- **Shanghai** (Yangtze River Delta; SAIC, Geely, NIO)
- **Guangzhou / Shenzhen** (Pearl River Delta; BYD Shenzhen factory, many used vehicle traders)
- **Chongqing** (Western China; Chery, Lifan; exports typically road-transferred to Tianjin or Shanghai)

Factory-to-port transit varies by OEM logistics and includes temporary parking at the export terminal while:
- Documentation is prepared and verified
- Battery state of charge is confirmed
- Pre-shipment inspection (if required) is conducted
- Export customs declaration is filed and approved

For vehicles purchased through grey-market channels, this phase can extend significantly — particularly after China's January 2026 180-day rule, which requires OEM documentation that may take time to obtain or verify.

### Phase 2: Ocean Transit (China → African Port)
**Typical duration: 20–35 days (Suez routing), 35–50 days (Cape routing)**

The ocean transit phase is the most quoted and the most variable. Under normal routing via the Suez Canal:

| Origin Port | Destination Port | Estimated Transit |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Tianjin | Djibouti | 22–28 days |
| Shanghai | Djibouti | 20–26 days |
| Guangzhou | Djibouti | 19–25 days |
| Tianjin | Mombasa | 25–32 days |
| Shanghai | Mombasa | 23–30 days |
| Guangzhou | Mombasa | 22–28 days |
| Shanghai | Dar es Salaam | 25–32 days |

**Red Sea routing note**: Since late 2023, Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping have pushed several major carriers to route via the Cape of Good Hope. This adds 14–21 days and $200–$500 per vehicle in freight cost. As of mid-2026, routing varies by carrier; confirm with your freight forwarder at booking.

Transit times are **port-to-port** — they do not include berth waiting time at the destination port, which can add 1–5 days during peak periods.

### Phase 3: Port Clearance at African Destination
**Typical duration: 5–15 working days**

Port clearance is the phase most frequently underestimated by importers. The time from vessel berthing to vehicle release depends on:
- Completeness and accuracy of documentation submitted
- Whether a physical inspection is triggered (random or documentary discrepancy)
- Port congestion at the time of arrival
- Whether the importer has submitted documents pre-arrival (recommended)

Approximate clearance times by port:

| Port | Documentation Complete | With Physical Inspection | Peak Congestion |
|------|----------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| Djibouti | 5–8 days | 10–14 days | +3–5 days |
| Mombasa | 5–10 days | 10–15 days | +3–7 days |
| Dar es Salaam | 7–12 days | 12–18 days | +5–10 days |

**Pre-arrival submission** significantly compresses clearance time. Filing your Bill of Lading, Commercial Invoice, Certificate of Origin, and country-specific permits before the vessel arrives allows customs processing to begin ahead of physical release, cutting 2–4 working days off clearance.

### Phase 4: Port to Destination (Inland Transit)
**Typical duration: 1–5 days**

For vehicles not destined for port cities:

| Origin Port | Destination | Mode | Estimated Transit |
|-------------|-------------|------|-------------------|
| Djibouti | Addis Ababa | Road (900 km) | 1–3 days |
| Djibouti | Addis Ababa | Rail (756 km) | 2–3 days |
| Mombasa | Nairobi | Road / SGR (480 km) | 1–2 days |
| Mombasa | Kampala | Road (800 km) | 2–3 days |
| Mombasa | Kigali | Road (1,300 km) | 3–5 days |
| Dar es Salaam | Lusaka | Road (1,900 km) | 3–5 days |

Road trucking is the dominant mode for vehicle transport. The Standard Gauge Railway (SGR) Mombasa–Nairobi is used for container freight but not commonly for individual vehicle transport.

### Phase 5: Pre-Delivery Inspection (PDI) and Dealer Preparation
**Typical duration: 1–3 days**

Upon arrival at the dealership, vehicles require:
- Physical inspection for transit damage
- Battery health check (BMS readout)
- Software update check (Chinese EV OTA updates may need WiFi connectivity)
- Cleaning and detailing
- Any local fitment (number plates, accessories)

## Complete Timeline Summary

| Phase | Low | Typical | High |
|-------|-----|---------|------|
| Factory to export port | 3 days | 5 days | 10 days |
| Ocean transit (Suez routing) | 19 days | 26 days | 35 days |
| Ocean transit (Cape routing) | 35 days | 44 days | 55 days |
| Port clearance | 5 days | 8 days | 18 days |
| Inland transit | 1 day | 2 days | 5 days |
| PDI and prep | 1 day | 2 days | 3 days |
| **Total (Suez)** | **29 days** | **43 days** | **71 days** |
| **Total (Cape)** | **45 days** | **55 days** | **91 days** |

**Practical planning figure**: budget **50–60 days** total for a China-to-Addis Ababa or China-to-Nairobi shipment under normal conditions in 2026. Build in a 2-week buffer for first-time orders while you calibrate your specific supplier and freight forwarder's timeline.

## Working Capital Implication

At the typical mid-point timeline of 50–60 days:
- Payment terms with Chinese suppliers are typically 30–50% deposit at order, balance before BL release
- Working capital is committed 50–60 days before a vehicle is available for sale
- For dealers operating on thin margins, this is the single largest operational cash flow challenge

Options to compress the cash cycle:
- **Pre-order from a local importer** with existing inventory (adds margin but eliminates the waiting period)
- **Negotiate BL-against-payment terms** to delay final payment to port arrival rather than loading
- **Submit documents pre-arrival** to cut clearance time by up to a week
