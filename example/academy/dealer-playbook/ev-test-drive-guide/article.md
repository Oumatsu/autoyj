## Overview

An EV test drive is not the same as an ICE test drive — and dealers who run it the same way leave most of the conversion opportunity on the table. The EV's advantages (instant torque, silence, smoothness, single-pedal driving) are things a customer must experience physically to believe. No amount of specification recitation produces the same effect as 30 seconds of pulling away from a traffic light in an EV for the first time. This guide gives you a structured test drive process designed specifically for African urban conditions and customers who are largely unfamiliar with electric vehicles, so you can turn every test drive into a high-probability close.

---

## Why the EV Test Drive Is Your Best Closer

Before covering process, understand the psychology.

When a customer books a test drive, they have already passed through the awareness and consideration stages. Their objections (battery, charging, price) are front of mind. But objections are largely rational — and the EV's most compelling advantages are largely sensory. Instant torque, zero engine noise, and smooth acceleration are not things you can argue your way to. They have to be felt.

**What happens in most EV test drives that go wrong:**
- The salesperson talks too much during the drive, competing with the vehicle's own impression
- The route is the same as the ICE test route — highway loops where EVs have no particular advantage
- The customer never experiences regen braking, because no one explained it
- The salesperson gets back in the showroom and immediately starts talking about price

**What happens in test drives that close:**
- The salesperson sets up the experience before it begins
- The route includes a standing start, some stop-go urban driving, and a moment of regen
- The salesperson is mostly quiet during the drive and lets the vehicle do the selling
- The debrief focuses on what the customer just felt, not on specifications

## Before the Drive: The 5-Minute Setup

The setup conversation before the customer gets behind the wheel is as important as the drive itself. It primes the customer to notice the right things.

**Step 1: Explain what is different before they start**

"Before you drive, let me tell you about two things that will feel different from your petrol car. First: when you press the accelerator, the power is instant — there's no delay while a gear changes or an engine revs up. You'll feel it immediately. Second: when you lift your foot from the accelerator, the car slows down on its own — that's regenerative braking, which is the car recovering energy. You can control how strong this is. Most drivers love it once they're used to it."

This briefing does two things: it prevents the customer being startled or confused by the driving feel, and it directs their attention to the sensations that will be most impressive.

**Step 2: Show them the range display**

Point to the range indicator before you start. "It shows [X] km of range right now. After our test drive, we'll check it again — you'll be able to see how little energy a short drive actually uses. Most people are surprised."

This plants a question in their mind that creates engagement during and after the drive.

**Step 3: Let them set up the seat and mirrors**

This sounds trivial but it matters. A customer who adjusts the car to their body is already treating it as theirs. Do not rush this.

## The Route: What to Include

Design your test drive route around the EV's advantages in African urban conditions. You have approximately 15–20 minutes. Use them deliberately.

**Segment 1: The standing start (first 60 seconds)**

Find a section of road where the customer can accelerate from a complete stop. Instruct them: "When the road is clear, press the accelerator firmly — not all the way, just a firm press."

Let them experience instant torque. Do not say anything for 10 seconds after the acceleration. Let the moment settle. Then: "That's what makes these cars so useful in city traffic — the response is immediate every time."

**Segment 2: Stop-go urban driving (8–10 minutes)**

This is where EVs genuinely outperform ICE in African cities. Stop-go traffic that would have an ICE driver changing gears constantly is entirely smooth in an EV — no clutch, no gear changes, no stalling, no overheating in slow traffic. Let the customer experience this for several minutes without commentary.

If they are comfortable, have them try lifting the foot off the accelerator more aggressively to feel regen braking. "Try lifting your foot completely — see how the car slows down? You can drive through city traffic using the accelerator alone for most of it. Some drivers barely touch the brake pedal."

**Segment 3: Silence moment (1–2 minutes)**

At a slower moment during city driving, ask them to switch off the radio if it is on. "Notice how quiet it is?" A genuinely quiet car interior is something many customers have never experienced and it leaves a strong impression.

**Segment 4: The return and range check**

When you return to the showroom, reference the range indicator again. "We drove about [X] km and used [Y] km of range. How does that compare to what you expected?"

This closes a loop you opened in the setup and gives the customer a concrete data point: the vehicle uses less energy than they assumed.

## During the Drive: What to Say and What Not to Say

**Say:**
- Contextual prompts that draw attention to what they're experiencing ("Notice the silence at this speed")
- Brief questions that keep them engaged ("How does the acceleration feel compared to what you expected?")
- Honest range contextualisation if they ask ("In Addis at this altitude, most owners see around [adjusted figure] — I'll explain why after the drive")

**Do not say:**
- Specification recitation ("This model has 204 horsepower and a 64.4 kWh battery")
- Competitor comparisons during the drive
- Anything about price
- Long explanations that require the customer to split attention between listening and driving

The vehicle is doing the selling during the drive. Your role is to facilitate and occasionally punctuate, not narrate.

---

## The Debrief: Converting the Experience to a Decision

When you return to the showroom, sit down before discussing anything else. The debrief conversation, done well, moves naturally toward a close.

**Opening question:** "What was your strongest impression from the drive?"

Let them answer. Most customers will mention either the torque, the silence, or the regen braking. Whatever they mention, affirm it and connect it to their stated daily use.

"You mentioned the city driving feel. That's the same every day — stop-go traffic in Addis is where this car really shows its difference. No gear changes, no engine noise, no stalling."

**Transition to practicalities:** "The two things people usually want to clarify after a test drive are range and charging. Based on your daily distance, I can show you exactly what the running cost looks like — do you want to go through those numbers now?"

This moves from the sensory experience to the rational justification — following the customer's natural decision-making sequence rather than fighting it.

---

## Handling the Customer Who Is Impressed But Not Ready to Decide

Some customers will be genuinely impressed by the drive but still not ready to commit. This is a legitimate position — EV is a new category and they may need more time.

**The correct response:** Give them something concrete to take away, and set a specific next step.

"I'll send you a summary on WhatsApp — the range expectation for your commute, the monthly running cost comparison, and the current pricing. When you've had a chance to look at it, let me know if you have questions. Are you free to talk again on [specific day]?"

Do not let the interaction end without a specific next touchpoint. A test drive that ends with "let me know if you decide" is a test drive that likely ends in silence.
