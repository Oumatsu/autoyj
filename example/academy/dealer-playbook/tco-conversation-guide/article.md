## Overview

The total cost of ownership (TCO) conversation is the most powerful tool an EV dealer has in Africa. Because EV sticker prices are often higher than equivalent ICE vehicles, customers who evaluate only the purchase price will frequently choose the ICE option. The dealers who consistently close EV deals are those who can rapidly and confidently shift the conversation from upfront cost to operating cost over time. This guide gives you the calculation framework, the numbers, and the exact language to use with Ethiopian customers.

---

## Why TCO Works as a Sales Conversation

A customer who objects to an EV's price is not objecting to the vehicle — they are objecting to an incomplete financial picture. When you provide the complete picture — monthly fuel savings, oil service elimination, reduced brake maintenance — most buyers can see that the "expensive" option is actually the economical one over their expected holding period.

The key insight: **Ethiopian buyers are highly cost-conscious and make rational financial decisions**. This works in your favour once you show them the real numbers. The TCO conversation respects their intelligence and gives them data they can take home and verify.

## The Core TCO Components

An EV's total cost of ownership advantage over an ICE vehicle comes from five categories:

**1. Fuel vs electricity cost**
The single largest monthly saving. Ethiopian petrol prices have fluctuated significantly with the Birr's depreciation, but electricity tariffs have been more stable. The per-kilometre cost gap between petrol and grid electricity is substantial.

**2. Engine oil and filter elimination**
ICE vehicles require oil changes every 5,000–7,500 km, typically costing ETB 2,000–3,500 per service. EVs have no engine oil. This is a fixed, recurring saving that accumulates predictably.

**3. Transmission maintenance elimination**
Automatic transmissions require fluid changes. Manual transmissions require clutch replacement (ETB 8,000–15,000 every 80,000–150,000 km typically). EVs have single-speed reduction gears with minimal maintenance requirements.

**4. Brake maintenance reduction**
Regenerative braking in EVs significantly extends brake pad life. In urban Ethiopian driving (Addis Ababa traffic), where ICE brakes are constantly in use, EV regen can recover a substantial portion of deceleration energy while reducing physical brake wear. ICE brake pad sets (all four wheels) cost ETB 3,000–6,000 and are often needed every 30,000–50,000 km in city use. EV pads typically last 60,000–100,000 km or more.

**5. Exhaust and emissions system elimination**
Catalytic converters, mufflers, and exhaust system components are absent in EVs. These are significant repair costs in ICE vehicles (ETB 5,000–20,000+ for major exhaust work).

## How to Present This in a Sales Conversation

Structure matters. Do not open with a spreadsheet. Build to it.

**Step 1: Establish the holding period**
"How long do you typically keep a car before you sell or replace it?"

If the answer is 3–5 years or more, the TCO calculation is strongly in the EV's favour. If the answer is "less than 2 years," the savings are smaller and the conversation shifts to resale value.

**Step 2: Establish the usage pattern**
"How many kilometres do you typically drive in a week?"

Higher mileage = larger monthly fuel saving = stronger TCO argument. Fleet operators and taxi drivers who cover 150–200+ km/day have the most compelling TCO case.

**Step 3: Introduce the fuel comparison first**
Start with the single largest saving — fuel vs electricity. Keep it in monthly terms, not annual (monthly feels tangible).

"At your driving level, you would probably spend around ETB [X] per month on petrol in that ICE car. In the EV, your electricity cost for the same distance is around ETB [Y]. That's a monthly saving of ETB [Z]."

**Step 4: Add the service savings**
"On top of that, EVs don't need oil changes. That's another ETB [X] per month you get to keep."

**Step 5: State the payback in practical terms**
"So over 3 years of driving at your level, the total savings are roughly ETB [total]. The EV's price premium over the petrol car is ETB [premium]. The savings more than cover the difference within [timeframe]."

**Step 6: Offer the worksheet**
"Would it help if I worked out your specific numbers based on your actual driving and the current petrol price?" Then do it — on paper or a simple phone calculator — while the customer watches. Transparency closes deals.

## Handling the Birr Depreciation Factor

Ethiopian customers are aware that the Birr has lost significant value against the dollar over the past three years (it depreciated approximately 60% against the USD between 2023 and 2025, according to National Bank of Ethiopia data). Some will worry that electricity costs will also inflate.

**The honest answer:** Electricity tariffs in Ethiopia are set by the Ethiopian Energy Authority and have historically been more stable than imported-commodity prices (petrol is USD-denominated and directly tracks Birr depreciation). Petrol prices in Ethiopia have risen sharply as the Birr weakened. Grid electricity has risen less dramatically.

**The redirect:** "Petrol prices go up when the Birr weakens because petrol is imported and paid for in dollars. Electricity is produced domestically — from the hydropower plants Ethiopia already owns. It's not directly exposed to exchange rate risk in the same way."

This is a genuinely strong argument in Ethiopia's context and most customers find it compelling when explained clearly.

## Fleet Buyer TCO: A Different Conversation

Fleet operators — taxi associations, ride-hail companies, NGO fleets, government vehicles — are the highest-value TCO conversation because their mileage is extreme. A taxi in Addis covers 200–300 km per day. At that usage level, the fuel saving alone can be ETB 15,000–25,000 per month per vehicle.

For fleet buyers, present the TCO differently:

1. Start with fleet size (number of vehicles)
2. Calculate total monthly fuel bill across the fleet for ICE
3. Calculate total monthly electricity cost for the same fleet in EV
4. Present the monthly fleet saving
5. Show the payback period on the per-unit premium

A fleet operator managing 20 vehicles who sees ETB 300,000–500,000 in monthly fuel savings presented clearly is not negotiating over the sticker price. They are asking when they can take delivery.

## Common Mistakes in the TCO Conversation

**Using annual numbers instead of monthly:** Annual figures (ETB 84,000 in fuel) feel abstract. Monthly figures (ETB 7,000 per month) feel like rent or salary — immediate and real.

**Using factory range figures for fuel consumption calculations:** Ethiopian urban driving at altitude uses more fuel per kilometre than manufacturer test figures. Use conservative assumptions that will be verified by the customer's real experience.

**Forgetting to ask about the holding period:** A customer who plans to sell in 12 months has a fundamentally different TCO equation than one planning to own for 5 years. Tailor the argument to the actual plan.

**Making up numbers:** If you don't know the current petrol price, look it up. If you're not sure of the electricity tariff tier the customer will fall into, say so. A TCO conversation that falls apart because of an incorrect number destroys far more trust than simply not having the number ready.
