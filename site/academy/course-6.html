Module 01

Equipment Procurement

Overview

Setting up an EV service centre requires specialized equipment that differs significantly from traditional ICE workshops. The right tools not only enable proper diagnostics and repairs but also ensure technician safety when working with high-voltage systems.

Essential Equipment Categories
  • Lifting Systems: Insulated hydraulic lifts rated for EV curb weights, with battery pack removal capabilities.
  • Diagnostic Machines: OEM-level scan tools capable of reading BMS, motor controller, and thermal system data.
  • Battery Handling Equipment: Insulated battery drop tables, HV disconnect tools, and certified storage racks.
  • Thermal Management Tools: Coolant fill/vacuum stations, thermal imaging cameras, and insulation testers.
  • Safety Gear: HV-rated insulated gloves (Class 0), arc flash face shields, insulating mats, and lockout/tagout kits.

Case Example

A new service centre in Egypt reduced downtime by 40% after installing recommended diagnostic and lifting equipment. The investment paid for itself within 8 months through faster service turnaround.

Module 02

Technician Training

Overview

Well-trained technicians are the backbone of any successful EV service centre. Unlike ICE vehicles where mechanical experience transfers directly, EV service requires a combination of high-voltage safety knowledge, software diagnostic skills, and battery system expertise.

  • Battery Safety: High-voltage disconnect procedures, emergency response to thermal events, and proper PPE usage.
  • Electrical Systems: HV component identification, inverter and DC-DC converter diagnostics, and insulation testing.
  • Software Diagnostics: BMS data interpretation, CAN bus analysis, and firmware update procedures.
  • Customer Service: EV-specific consultation skills — explaining range, charging, and battery health to owners.

Case Example

A Nigerian dealership trained 12 technicians through a structured 6-week program combining classroom theory and hands-on practice. Within 3 months, their EV service bay was handling 40+ vehicles per month with zero safety incidents.

Module 03

Parts Inventory Management

Overview

Effective parts inventory management is critical for minimizing vehicle downtime and maximizing service revenue. EV spare parts differ significantly from ICE — the high-value items are battery modules, inverters, and chargers rather than alternators, starters, and exhaust systems.

  • Critical Stock: Maintain minimum stock levels for high-turnover items: cabin filters, brake pads, wiper blades, 12V auxiliary batteries.
  • High-Value Items: Battery modules, drive motors, and onboard chargers — stock based on local fleet composition.
  • Inventory System: Implement barcode tracking with automated reorder points and supplier lead time monitoring.
  • Supplier Relationships: Establish direct accounts with Chinese OEM parts departments and authorized distributors.

Case Example

A Tanzanian service centre reduced average repair time from 5 days to 36 hours by implementing a structured inventory system with min/max levels and weekly supplier orders.

Module 04

Warranty Claim Processes

Overview

Warranty claim handling is a key differentiator for EV dealerships. Chinese EV manufacturers typically offer 5-8 year battery warranties and 3-5 year vehicle warranties, but the claims process requires meticulous documentation and understanding of OEM-specific procedures.

  • Documentation: Maintain detailed service records including diagnostic logs, photos of faulty components, and replacement part serial numbers.
  • Claim Categories: Understand the difference between manufacturing defects, wear-and-tear exclusions, and damage claims.
  • Submission Timelines: Most OEMs require claim submission within 7-14 days of diagnosis — missed deadlines mean absorbed costs.
  • Approval Workflow: Establish a clear escalation path from technician diagnosis to service manager review to OEM claim submission.

Case Example

A dealership in Ghana improved their warranty claim approval rate from 45% to 92% within 6 months by implementing a standardized documentation checklist and training all technicians on OEM-specific photography requirements.
