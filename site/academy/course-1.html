Module 01

Battery Chemistry: LFP vs NMC

Overview

The battery is the single most expensive and most important component in any electric vehicle. Understanding the two dominant chemistries is essential for every sales professional.

LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate)
  • Pros: High safety, long cycle life, lower cost.
  • Cons: Lower energy density, slightly heavier.
NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt)
  • Pros: High energy density, suitable for long-range/high-performance vehicles.
  • Cons: Higher cost, thermal stability challenges.

Case Example

Urban delivery EVs in African cities often prioritize LFP for durability and safety. Luxury EV SUVs targeting long-range trips often use NMC to maximize range.

Module 02

Motor Types

Overview

Electric vehicles use different motor types depending on their performance goals, cost targets, and operating environment. Knowing the difference helps you match the right vehicle to the right customer.

Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motor (PMSM)
  • High efficiency, compact design, low maintenance.
  • Ideal for passenger EVs where efficiency and performance are valued.
Induction Motor (IM)
  • Robust, simpler design, slightly lower efficiency.
  • Suitable for heavy-duty fleet EVs in environments with limited maintenance infrastructure.

Case Example

PMSM is recommended for EVs sold in regions where efficiency and performance are valued over initial cost. IM is suitable for heavy-duty fleet EVs in environments with limited maintenance infrastructure.

Module 03

Charging Standards

Overview

Charging infrastructure is a top concern for EV buyers. Understanding the different standards and their regional compatibility is critical for addressing customer questions.

AC Charging (Slow/Medium)
  • Suitable for overnight or workplace charging.
  • Uses standard Type 2 or GB/T connectors depending on the market.
DC Fast Charging
  • Reduces charging time to 30-60 minutes for long-distance trips.
  • Essential for fleet operators with high daily mileage.
CHAdeMO vs CCS
  • Standardized connectors with regional compatibility variations.
  • Chinese EVs typically use GB/T standard — adapters are available for African markets.

Case Example

In African urban areas, AC charging may suffice for daily commuting, while DC fast charging is recommended for intercity travel and fleet operations.

Module 04

Explaining Range Anxiety

Overview

Range anxiety is the #1 objection first-time EV buyers raise. Your ability to address this concern confidently can make or break the sale.

Range anxiety is the concern that an EV may run out of battery before reaching a charging station. Mitigation strategies include battery management systems, trip planning applications, and access to fast-charging networks.

Case Example

A 400 km-range EV easily covers daily commutes of 50-100 km, reducing range anxiety. In most African cities, the average daily commute is under 80 km — meaning even a 300 km EV provides a 3-4 day buffer between charges.
